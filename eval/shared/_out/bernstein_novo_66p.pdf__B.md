### FUNDAMENTALS DEEP DIVE: NOVO NORDISK (UP; UNDERPERFORM); WHY’S THE KNIFE STILL FALLING?

WHY DO WE PROJECT BELOW AVERAGE GROWTH?

We project Novo Nordisk to deliver sales growth below EU biopharma peers 2026-2031 inclusive (Exhibit 1) for two reasons:

1/Semaglutide loss of exclusivity: In 2025, products containing semaglutide (Ozempic, Rybelsus, and Wegovy) accounted for 74% of Novo’s group sales. Novo has hitched its growth prospects on the Wegovy Pill (oral semaglutide) and CagriSema (cagrilintide + semaglutide) over the medium term. Incorporating loss of exclusivity (LOE) in key international markets in 2026 (Canada, Brazil, India, and China), and compound patent expiries in the EU and US in 2031 and 2032 (respectively), we estimate that 1/3 of group sales are at risk of generic competition in 2026, rising to 2/3 in 2032 (Exhibit 3).

2/Weakening positions in obesity and diabetes markets: Having entered the GLP-1 market for both diabetes (2017, through Ozempic) and obesity (2021, through Wegovy), Novo’s semaglutide has lost considerable market share to Lilly in both diabetes (vs Mounjaro) and obesity (vs Zepbound). We think these dynamics are entrenched and see no prospect of a share reversal. We forecast -12% group sales growth in 2026 (driven by US price deflation) and then anemic growth across to 2033 (Exhibit 1).

## Novo’s Earnings Story so Far: Meteoric Rise, Unfortunate Fall

Why did Novo deliver a sustained period of earnings upgrades? To recap, Novo was an early player in the GLP-1 market, with Victoza (active ingredient liraglutide) gaining FDA approval in 2010. This was the first once-daily human GLP-1 approved for type 2 diabetes.

1. GLP1 penetration of US diabetes market. Novo launched Ozempic in the US in 2017, a once weekly GLP-1 for type 2 diabetes management. Since then, GLP-1s penetration in the US diabetes market has steadily increased (Exhibit 9). In our view this is primarily because, unlike insulin, these products lead to weight loss rather than weight gain.

2. Inflection of the US obesity market for GLP-1s. The weight-loss benefits enjoyed by patients on Ozempic increased investors’ expectations of the potential for GLP-1s to be used as broader weight management medications - with many patients using Ozempic “off-label” (i.e. for weight-loss and not for diabetes management). Subsequently, data from the phase 3 SELECT outcomes trial in 3Q23 inflected perceptions of the potential of GLP-1s in weight loss. This is because in SELECT Wegovy lowered cardiovascular risk (a composite of reducing heart attacks, strokes, and other CV related deaths) by 20%. This proved that GLP-1s deliver medicinal value beyond weight-loss.

### What has caused this downgrade cycle? There have been three major events in our view:

• Poor execution on manufacturing. We think Novo’s strategy for semaglutide production is a prime study for “failure to prepare is preparation to fail”. Here is a recap of key events:

• Novo let demand for semaglutide products (namely Ozempic and Wegovy) run ahead of their (known) installed capacity for quality checking and fill-finishing. In March 2022, the FDA announced a shortage of semaglutide, under section 503b. This led to compounding pharmacies entering the US market to ease the semaglutide shortage.

• Novo has invested $25bn since 2022 in capex to bolster fill-finish (putting the active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) into pen injection devices). Then, in early 2025, the FDA announced that the semaglutide shortage was over. However, compounding pharmacies have proven very difficult to remove from the market. Our channel checks indicate are these drugs still command 1/3 of the US market on a volume basis.

• We think Novo has suffered heavily on three fronts: A/loss of Ozempic and Wegovy sales 2022-present, B/greater than expected capex investments from a position of relative weakness, and C/ allowing competitors (compounders and Lilly) to take share.

• Three profit warnings in 2025 and another one in 2026 (Exhibit 10). Novo cut its sales and EBIT growth guidance for FY25 three times across 2025 (at 1Q, 2Q, and 3Q), citing lower penetration of GLP-1s in the US (1Q), in international markets (2Q), and the slower than expected exit of compounders (1Q, 2Q and 3Q). Management also announced a large restructuring program in early September 2025, cutting 9k jobs. Then the first wave of public channel GLP-1 price cuts in the US led by The White House in November contributed to the guidance cut at 3Q25. Novo is now guiding for negative group sales growth at constant currency (in the 5-13% range) in 2026 because of more US price cuts needed to stimulate long term volume growth.

• CagriSema disappointment(s) eroding confidence in the pipeline. CagriSema was initially billed as Novo’s answer to tirzepatide (active ingredient in Lilly’s Mounjaro (diabetes) and Zepbound (obesity)). More specifically, Novo had hoped CagriSema (cagrilintide (amylin) + semaglutide) could achieve 25% weight loss. (For context, Zepbound delivered 21%, placebo adjusted weight loss, in the phase 3 SURMOUNT-1 obesity trials). But in REDEFINE-1 (which read out December 2024), CagriSema disappointed investors, achieving 20.4% placebo adjusted weight-loss. In REDEFINE-4, which was a head-to-head trial of CagriSema vs Zepbound (which read out in February 2026) the former failed to meet its primary endpoint of non-inferiority (i.e. it was inferior to Zepbound for weight loss). We believe this chain of events has dented investors’ confidence in Novo’s R&D execution.

# Why else aren’t we excited about the late stage pipeline?

In the short term Novo Nordisk intends to launch Mim8 for use hemophilia A. These patients are at risk of (potentially fatal) uncontrolled bleeding events because they cannot produce enough Factor VIII (protein which facilitates blood clotting) on their own. Mim8 has the same mechanism of action as Roche’s Hemlibra (launched in 2017; 2025 sales $5bn).

Once administered to hemophiliacs, Hemlibra behaves like naturally occurring Factor VIII (the drug is a mimetic). Mim8 has the same bispecific antibody mechanism of action (in effect the drug’s ‘two keys’ fit into two different padlocks inside the body) and was invented by Genmab (UP). Novo in-licensed this drug in 2015.

We acknowledge that from the ‘get go’ Mim8 will be available in a prefilled syringe (PFS) which can be directly injected under the skin (subcutaneous formulation). Hemlibra is also administered subcutaneously but via vial and syringe so is arguably less convenient. However, we believe that Hemlibra’s 9 years of safe use represents a formidable barrier to entry to Mim8. We also

## US OBESITY MARKET: SOLID VOLUME GROWTH OFFSETTING STICKY DEFLATION

The obesity market is already worth $25bn in 2025. We forecast a 12% value CAGR over the next decade, where the US obesity market will be worth $85bn (Exhibit 42). So far, this story has been dominated by the share battle between Novo and Lilly (O; covered by Courtney Breen). But even with new entrants arriving at the end of the decade, we believe the market will remain a duopoly, with Lilly the dominant player (Exhibit 43).

Our key takeaway from the past five years is simple: in injectables, weight loss wins. Tolerance profile and whether the drug comes with restrictions (e.g. fasting before taking it) are of secondary importance - headline weight loss is the primary needle mover. We think this is largely for two reasons.

1. Obesity drugs are prescribed in primary care settings, where practitioners are relatively time poor. Practitioners will prescribe their “favored” GLP-1 and only move patients onto a different product should their side effects become intolerable.

2. The direct-to-consumer channel in the US has increased the importance of headline weight loss. Patients seeking to lose weight (reasonably) want the product which maximizes the speed and magnitude of their weight loss. The tolerance profile is a “second order” consideration, whereby a degree of discomfort is tolerated for additional weight loss. (Proven ‘outcomes’ benefits such as stroke reduction are even further down the list).

We think the market dynamics are tough to disrupt. We don’t foresee a share reversal back to 2023 or 2024 levels (where Novo had 60% of the US obesity market). Nor do we think a major disruptor is waiting in the wings. Instead, we think Lilly will remain the dominant player, Novo the (distant) number two, and a tail of players with smaller shares. In our view, both Lilly and Novo should benefit from existing positions on the market, which should be protected by pipeline launches.

### APPENDIX 1: RARE DISEASE

In 3Q25, Novo Nordisk confirmed its acquisition of Akero therapeutics for ~$5.2Bn in cash and contingent value rights to gain access to its lead asset efruxifermin. The drug is a bivalent fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) and beta-klotho receptor analogue used in the treatment of Metabolic-dysfunction Associated Steato-Hepatitis (MASH). MASH is a disease of the liver with a high unmet clinical need, and we believe efruxifermin will be a best-in class drug.

What is MASH?

Metabolic-dysfunction Associated Steato-Hepatitis (MASH), previously known as Non-Alcoholic Steato-Hepatitis (NASH), is a disease process involving the build up of excess fatty tissues within the liver. The fat is deposited through a variety of biological processes, and can irritate healthy liver cells and cause inflammation and scarring to build up.

Scarring in the liver is more broadly known as fibrosis, or cirrhosis in severe cases which can significantly increase the risk of liver cancer. MASH can cause fibrosis, and although the two are strongly linked are strictly speaking separate. Hence, one can exist without the other.

To diagnose MASH, physicians may use bloodwork to examine liver function tests, ultrasound scans, and (rarely) a biopsy (removal of a sample via surgery) of the liver are conducted to confirm the disease pathology. With progress in ultrasound technology and protocols, the need for biopsy has reduced significantly and clinicians are growing increasingly confident in treating the disease in the absence of a confirmed tissue sample.

In terms of treatment, a healthy diet and physical activity is often the first line to minimize circulating fats in the bloodstream, and mitigate the metabolic risk factors contributing to MASH.

Bariatric surgery (forced stomach shrinkage by decreasing the diameter of the gastric tract) to treat obesity is evidenced to be up to 90% effective in treating MASH. Meanwhile, liver transplantation can be offered in cases of MASH where liver function is most severely restricted.

Medical (i.e. drug) treatment usually starts with cholesterol lowering medication and good diabetic control. Although medical treatment specific to MASH is uncommon with only a few approved agents licensed for the disease.

## Current therapeutic landscape

Rezdiffra (Madrigal therapeutics; unrated) is a drug that mimics the thyroid hormone to promote fat breakdown and dissolution. More specifically, Rezdiffra binds to the thyroid hormone receptor beta (THR-beta) in liver fat to promote its breakdown.

In the phase III MAESTRO-NASH trial program, Rezdiffra was compared to placebo to assess fibrosis and MASH on ultrasound imaging. 25.9% of patients achieved at least a 1 stage improvement in their F score without worsening of MASH compared to 14.2% in the placebo group (p<0.001). No concerning safety signals were reported and the drug was approved as the first ever licensed treatment exclusively for MASH in 1Q2024. Following sales of $180m in 2024, consensus (Bloomberg) projects Rezdiffra peak sales of $5Bn in 2031 for a CAGR (25-31e) of 61%.

The second, and only other medication with an indication in MASH is semaglutide, known as Wegovy (Novo Nordisk; O), an analogue of the glucagon like peptide 1 (GLP-1) receptor. This drug acts on the hormone pathway that promotes appetite suppression, lowers blood sugar levels and improves control of body weight. Originally indicated for tackling obesity, the phase III ESSENCE study trialed Wegovy against placebo in MASH patients, evaluating whether there was improvement in liver stiffness and fibrosis reflected on ultrasound imaging and bloodwork.

### Spotlight on efruxifermin

Efruxifermin is a member of a new class of MASH medications that mimic the activity of fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21 . ### EXHIBIT 99: Fibroblast growth factor 21 - master metabolic regulator

By providing an analogue that is longer acting than the hormone present in the body, efruxifermin prolongs and amplifies the positive effects of normally present FGF21. Some of these desirable effects include healing of the scar tissue in the liver, and slowing down the MASH disease process.

With MASH induced fibrosis in particular, FGF21 has demonstrated impressive potential in vitro (in a test tube). ### APPENDIX 2: A GENERALIST’S GUIDE TO GLP-1S: WITH A ZOOM IN ON SEMAGLUTIDE

The Main Mechanisms of Action

Obesity drugs, in general, target our “hunger-related” hormones. As the body’s digestive system (and it’s links with the brain) are extremely complicated, there are numerous hormones needed for the body to function. A drug (such as semaglutide; active ingredient in Wegovy) may only target a single hormone (and is termed a “single agonist”) others (such as tirzepatide, active ingredient in Zepbound) target two hormones (“double agonist”). Below we list the main mechanisms of actions:

• GLP-1 receptor agonist. Mimics the Glucagon-Like Peptide 1 (a hormone) by inhibiting the release of glucagon (hormone raising blood sugar levels) and stimulating the production of insulin (a hormone that lowers blood sugar levels) thus reducing hyperglycemia which leads to lower blood sugar levels. Furthermore, GLP-1s reduce hunger and slow the GI (gastro intestinal) food flow leading to a decrease in appetite.

• Amylin receptor agonist. Mimics amylin (hormone secreted with insulin) which inhibits glucagon secretion and affects gastric emptying plus food intake.

• GIP receptor agonist. Mimics the Glucose-dependant Insulinotropic Peptide (hormone) which stimulates insulin production and sensitivity leading to a better insulin penetration in the bloodstream, thus lowering blood sugar levels. The hormone also reduces stomach acid production, leading to a slow GI food flow resulting in a decrease in appetite.

• Glucagon receptor agonist. Mimics glucagon which increases blood glucose levels and breaks down lipids and amino acids. Exogenous intake of glucagon leads to increased energy expenditure with a lower food intake.

Side Effects and Tolerance

The most common side effects from GLP-1s are gastro-intestinal (GI) problems. The primary mechanism of action of GLP-1s is to alter the way our “hunger hormones” behave and interact with each other. It is, therefore, unsurprising that gut- related issues are the primary side effect from taking GLP-1s.

• Nausea, vomiting, constipation, bloating, and digestive reflux are all common GI adverse events. Especially towards the start of treatment, where the body’s digestive process is undergoing a “re-wiring”, side effects may manifest themselves. For most people, the side effects are manageable (or tolerated for the benefit of the weight loss).

• Fatigue, headaches, dizziness, and taste changes can also be caused by GLP-1 usage. As the body readjusts to a new level of caloric intake, the body undergoes a “metabolic stress test”. These side effects can be prominent when people are adjusting to new dosing levels, and can exacerbated by dehydration (GLP-1s also suppress the “thirst hormones”).

Whilst we think the tolerance profile of a drug is important, the main driver behind the success of an anti-obesity product remains headline the weight loss. Patients are willing to accept a worse tolerance profile in order to achieve larger amounts of weight loss. Most drugs (including those in the pipeline) have a similar discontinuation due to adverse events rates - certainly not different enough to shape patient behavior. We think the weight loss-tolerance trade off will become more important as more drugs enter the market, but weight-loss will remain the driving force.

Semaglutide: A Deep(er) Dive

Semaglutide is a long-acting glucagon-like peptide 1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA). Glucagon-like peptide-1 is a naturally occurring hormone that helps the body regulate blood sugar (glucose) levels, appetite, and the speed at which nutrients are moved from the stomach into the bloodstream.

Semaglutide is a modified version of the naturally occurring hormone. The GLP-1 drugs increase the length of time it lasts in the body and activates the GLP-1 receptors (starting signals) more steadily. Semaglutide has three primary effects on the body:

1. Pancreas: Increases the amount of insulin secreted when glucose is high (normally just after eating) but not when glucose levels are low (lowering the risk of hypoglycemic - crashes in blood sugar levels - attacks).

2. Stomach: Slows gastric emptying (slows the rate that nutrients enter the bloodstream) which “smooths” out the body’s glucose response. This lowers strain on the pancreas (reducing the risk of developing diabetes in the first instance, as well as slowing the pancreatic strain in diabetes patients) and helps patients feel “fuller” for longer.
