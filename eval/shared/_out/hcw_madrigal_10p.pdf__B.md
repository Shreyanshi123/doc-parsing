How does it alter our thesis and latest thinking? The revenue beat, the best NBRx month since the launch, and the continued steady patient additions directly validate our view that near-term headwinds are manageable as long-term factors such as expanding TAM, low penetration, and competitive coexistence remain important factors to be considered as one continues to think about the commercial program. Most importantly, we like Madrigal’s bullishness in developing combination therapies which was boosted by the clinical stage PNPLA3 asset acquisition. Management now has three distinct combination strategies, Ervogastat for broad-population enhanced steatosis reduction, MGL-2086 for pharmacologically inducing the weight loss that potentiates Rezdiffra's anti-fibrotic effect, and ARO-PNPLA3 for genotype-targeted precision therapy in a high-risk subpopulation. With respect to the development speed, we think Phase 2 studies might be NIT-based (primarily MRI-PDFF with fibrosis biomarker panels), and the bar for Phase 3 advancement is that combo data must be "meaningfully different" from Rezdiffra alone. We also note that the endocrinology expansion is an underappreciated growth driver. With over 10,000 prescribers now and GI outnumbering hepatology 10:1, the endo channel represents a largely untapped patient population, patients already on background GLP-1 therapy who still present with F2/F3 MASH.

What new questions have emerged? The DTC campaign is a new spending category with no established ROI in MASH, we need to see whether it's driving patient self-referrals or reinforcing awareness among already-diagnosed patients. Do endocrinology practices have diagnostic workflows to stage patients and initiate Rezdiffra. On the pipeline side, the key open question is what endpoints FDA will require for combination approvals?

What work do we anticipate doing? We plan to conduct KOL interviews focused on DTC effectiveness, endocrinologist receptivity to Rezdiffra, and clinical views on combination trial design, specifically what level of incremental efficacy over monotherapy would change prescribing practice.

What are we looking for going forward? EASL Congress (May 27-30), a MAESTRO-NASH/NAFLD1 secondary analysis on Lp(a)/LDL-C reduction (CV-risk read-through, statin-independent) and two real-world efficacy posters with up to 12 months of follow-up. MGL-2086 Phase 1 SAD initiation in 2Q, first clinical read on the oral GLP-1 strategy. Ervogastat DDI study initiation in 4Q 2026, confirms whether the fixed-dose oral combination pathway is viable. ARO-PNPLA3 regulatory engagement on Phase 2 design will signal whether FDA accepts a genotype-restricted approach. MAESTRO Outcomes remains on track for 2027, with positive data opens the nearly 245,000-patient F4c indication and makes Rezdiffra the first fully approved MASH therapy with outcomes data, well ahead of any competitor.

1Q26 in numbers. The company reported total revenues of $311.3M for 1Q26. Reported R&D expenses were $108.7M (inclusive of $54.3M in one-time upfront business development expenses), and SG&A expenses were $268.5M, driven by the endocrinology field force expansion and a new DTC campaign. Net loss was $94.4M, or ($3.25) per share. As of March 31, 2026, the company reported $817.9M in cash, cash equivalents, restricted cash, and marketable securities.

Valuation and Risks. We reiterate our $620 PT and our Buy rating. We estimate the intrinsic value of MDGL in our rNPV model as the sum of Rezdiffra in MASH with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (F2-F3) in both the U.S. and EU at $596 per share; and in MASH with compensated cirrhosis in both the U.S. and EU at $34.4 per share (75% PoS). Conservatively, we accrue zero terminal value beyond the 2033 projection period and assign no value to the combination pipeline (Ervogastat, MGL-2086, ARO- PNPLA3, or the six preclinical siRNA programs), which represents potential upside to our valuation as clinical data emerge. Key risks to our rating and PT include: (1) failure to obtain full FDA and EMA approvals across F2-F4c; (2) clinical trial failure or equivocal data in the MAESTRO-NASH Outcomes study; (3) meaningful delays to our expected timelines, including combination Phase 2 programs; (4) unforeseen competitive threats from GLP-1-based MASH entrants or other development compounds;
