### MASH Treatment Market Definition And Segmentations

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), previously known as non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), is a chronic and progressive liver disease characterized by fat accumulation, inflammation and fibrosis in the liver, primarily linked to obesity, insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes and other metabolic disorders. The primary purpose of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) treatment is to halt or reverse liver damage, prevent disease progression to cirrhosis or liver failure, and manage associated metabolic conditions. The treatment typically includes lifestyle interventions, pharmacotherapy, and in some cases, surgical options to reduce liver inflammation, fibrosis, and metabolic risk factors.i

The MASH treatment market consists of sales by entities (organizations, sole traders and partnerships) of MASH treatment drugs and therapy that is used when lifestyle modifications alone are insufficient to halt or reverse liver damage. It is utilized across a continuum of care, beginning with non-invasive interventions and progressing to pharmacological treatment or bariatric surgery in advanced cases.ii

Complementary interventions include nutritional counseling and physical activity programs, while substitutes may include broader metabolic treatments that indirectly address the condition. Treatment is administered in clinical settings such as hospitals and specialty clinics and increasingly through home-based care, especially for oral medications and telehealth-supported interventions.iii

Drugs are produced through biopharmaceutical and chemical synthesis processes involving small molecules and biologics, while therapies may encompass structured weight-loss programs or surgical interventions. These treatments are distributed through hospital pharmacies for complex or inpatient care, retail pharmacies for maintenance drugs and online pharmacies for chronic care management.iv

Sales occur through both institutional procurement and direct-to-consumer channels, depending on the healthcare infrastructure and insurance frameworks. Ongoing patient support involves regular liver function testing, imaging studies, biomarker monitoring and long-term care coordination by hepatologists, endocrinologists and primary care providers.v

The market covered in this report includes sales of MASH treatment by treatment such as drug and therapy. The market covered in this report includes sales of MASH treatment for various disease stages such as simple fatty liver / MASLD (no or mild fibrosis), early MASH (significant/advanced fibrosis), fibrotic MASH, MASH cirrhosis and liver failure / liver cancer mortality. The main end users of the market include hospitals, specialty clinics, homecare and other end-users.

This report excludes treatments specifically for liver conditions unrelated to metabolic dysfunction, such as alcoholic liver disease, viral hepatitis, autoimmune hepatitis and genetic liver disorders. It also excludes diagnostics not directly associated with treatment, experimental or unapproved therapies. Additionally, it does not include wellness or dietary supplements marketed without clinical evidence for MASH treatment.

### Advancements In Fibroblast Growth Factors (FGFs) Through Acquisitions

Major companies operating in the MASH treatment market are focusing on advancing fibroblast growth factor to strengthen their market position. Fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) is a hormone-like protein that regulates lipid, glucose and energy metabolism in the body. FGF21 analogs have shown promise by reducing liver fat, inflammation and fibrosis, thus improving liver health and helping to resolve MASH without worsening fibrosis. These effects make FGF21-based therapies a leading strategy for addressing the metabolic and fibrotic complications of MASH. For example, in May 2025, GlaxoSmithKline (GSK), a UK-based pharma company acquired Efimosfermin (also known as efimosfermin alfa), a once-monthly fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) from Boston Pharmaceuticals, a US-based pharma company. Efimosfermin is designed to provide direct antifibrotic action, aiming to reverse liver fibrosis and halt the progression of Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatohepatitis (MASH), including cases that have advanced to cirrhosis. In addition to MASH, the therapy is being developed for alcohol-related liver disease (ALD) and both forms of steatotic liver disease (SLD), broadening its potential impact on serious liver conditions. GSK plans to explore efimosfermin both as a monotherapy and in combination with other investigational agents, including their siRNA therapeutic for liver disease. The acquisition and development of efimosfermin position GSK as a significant player in the evolving market for innovative liver disease therapies.xxvii

### Focus On Receiving Government Approvals To Highlight Advancements in MASH

### Treatment

Major companies operating in the MASH treatment market are intensifying their efforts to secure regulatory approvals for their novel therapies. Achieving approvals from the government is a key strategy to accelerate development timelines and enhance market competitiveness. These approvals not only validate the clinical potential of their drug candidates but also position companies to capture early market share in a rapidly evolving therapeutic landscape. For example, in October 2024, Boehringer Ingelheim is a Germany-based pharmaceutical company actively involved in developing treatments for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), received approval from the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to their investigational drug survodutide for the treatment of adults with non- cirrhotic metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) and moderate to advanced fibrosis. This designation was awarded based on compelling Phase II clinical trial results that demonstrated significant improvements in liver health compared to existing therapies. Specifically, up to 83% of patients treated with survodutide showed a marked improvement in MASH, with a placebo-adjusted difference of 64.8%, alongside notable reductions in liver fibrosis. The Breakthrough Therapy status is intended to expedite the development and review process, reflecting the FDA’s recognition of survodutide’s potential to address a critical unmet medical need in MASH patients.xxxii
