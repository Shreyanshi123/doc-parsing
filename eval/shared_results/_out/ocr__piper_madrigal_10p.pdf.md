Patient numbers continue to grow where they are up 2.5x since 1Q25. Importantly, mgmt
emphasized the strength of REZDIFFRA uptake in practice where >42,250 patients are on drug

since 1Q25 which speaks to the high patient demand for an oral QD liver-targeted therapy

the continued growth of patients on drug demonstrates the utility of "wiring the system" where
REZDIFFRA continues to have 1L access establishing it as a foundational MASH therapy.

the continued addition of more patients every quarter. Moreover, mgmt noted this represents a
significant achievement by any standard especially in a new market where there is clear unmet
need and continued demand from both docs and patients (momentum is strong). Notably, mgmt
reminded us the US F2/F3 MASH population is substantially growing where it has increased by
~50% since YE23 and double-digit growth is expected moving forward (Exhibit 1). Accordingly,
key drivers of market expansion are overall increased disease awareness, increased diagnosis,
and increased patients under specialty care. For reference, mgmt noted NVO (not covered) has
helped to grow MASH awareness where MDGL is also seeing more interest from practices in
purchasing NITs (to facilitate staging of patients and ability to treat). Given this, mgmt emphasized
we are still in the early stages of the MASH market where the diagnosis rate is a little over ~10%
and penetration remains under ~10% which sets the stage for rapid expansion and a substantial
opportunity to blockbuster peak sales.

Combination of REZDIFFRA and GLP-1 usage continues to be ~25% where MDGL has
not observed an impact on REZDIFFRA's launch. When asked about the potential impact of
NVO's WEGOVY (semaglutide; MASH approval August 2025) on REZDIFFRA's launch, MDGL
indicated that they have observed no real difference or effect on the strength of launch. To support
this, MDGL indicated that they are exiting April with their best new-to-brand prescription numbers
since beginning REZDIFFRA's launch. Accordingly, mgmt discussed how GLP-1s are becoming
a background therapy where many patients are already on a GLP-1 or have had experience
with one prior to coming into the clinic for MASH treatment. As such, MDGL noted that ~25%
of patients are on a combination of REZDIFFRA and a GLP-1 and ~>50% of patients were
previously exposed to a GLP-1. Further, mgmt noted that they expect those numbers will continue
to rise given GLP-1's widespread use. Regarding payer dynamics for patients on combination
REZDIFFRA and GLP-1, MDGL pointed out that payers will cover a GLP-1 for use in a comorbid
indication such as obesity rather than covering both REZDIFFRA and a GLP-1 for the indication
of MASH. As a reminder, MDGL's pipeline includes MGL-2086 (oral GLP-1RA small molecule)
which has a Ph1 SAD study on track to initiate in 2Q26.

MDGL further expands pipeline with global licensing agreement with ARWR (covered
by Tenthoff) for ARO-PNPLA3, a clinical-stage siRNA. Additionally, MDGL highlighted the
recent exclusive global licensing agreement with ARWR for the clinical-stage ARO-PNPLA3.
Specifically, ARO-PNPLA3 is a GalNAc-conjugated siRNA designed to reduce expression of the
PNPLA3 gene which is highly prevalent among Hispanic MASH patients and represents ~30% of
moderate-to-advanced fibrosis patients (consistent with F2/F3). Accordingly, ARWR will receive
an upfront $25M payment with additional payments of up-to $975M if certain milestones are
achieved and royalties on net sales. In return, MDGL is granted the exclusive global license to
develop, manufacture, and commercialize ARO-PNPLAS. With this, it is crucial to appreciate
ARO-PNPLA3 already showed Ph1 (NCT04844450) PoC data in n=55 MAFLD patients who
were either homozygous or heterozygous carriers of the PNPLA3 1148M variant (~93% Hispanic/
Latino; Fabbrini E, et al. N Engl J Med. 2024 Aug 1;391(5):475-476). For reference, ARO-
PNPLA3 showed (Exhibit 4): 1) up-to -46% reductions in liver fat (MRI-PDFF) at Week 12
following a single-dose at the highest dose tested in PNPLA3 1148M homozygous patients; 2)
rapid onset of action with reductions observed as early as Week 6 and sustained response
through at-least Week 24; 3) strong safety/tolerability with no clinically meaningful AEs observed;
and 4) no liver fat content effect observed in heterozygous patients at any of the doses. Of note, a
second Ph1 (NCT05039710) in n=9 Japanese patients further support these data. Hence, ARO-
PNPLAS3 represents a de-risked clinical-stage asset with clear potential as a precision medicine
approach for patients at high-risk of MASH. When asked about what gave them confidence
in ARO-PNPLA3 to move forward on this deal, mgmt discussed its high potential to provide
complementary efficacy with REZDIFFRA. Specifically, MDGL highlighted their previous data
which supported that if there is a greater reduction in liver fat then there is greater efficacy
associated with REZDIFFRA. Given that ARO-PNPLA3 was able to drive up-to -46% liver
fat reduction with a clean safety/tolerability profile, MDGL believes there is strong potential
for additive efficacy with REZDIFFRA in the context of this subpopulation. Additionally, mgmt
reiterated this licensing agreement continues to support their growing R&D strategy where they
also gained the global rights to six pre-clinical siRNA programs in February 2026 (preclinical
development underway).
