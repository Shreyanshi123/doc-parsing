- 1. AACR to begin this weekend

The American Association for Cancer Research (AACR) used to be considered a pre-clinical and early-stage clinical oncology conference with relatively low significance. However, this year it is expected to be a highly important event that could lead a sector rebound depending on data results, as key domestic biotech firms are set to disclose core data. The conference is scheduled for Apr 17~22 (local time), and clinical abstract data is to be released early Saturday morning (KST).

Clinical data requiring particular attention includes that from: 1) Rznomics; and 2) HLB Innovation. Rznomics (RNA editing) and HLB Innovation (solid tumor CAR-T) are to present the first proof of concept for their respective platform technologies (proving efficacy and safety, respectively). Both are scheduled for oral presentations at the AACR—Rznomics on Apr 20 at 8:00 AM (KST) and HLB Innovation on Apr 21 at 2:00 (KST).

- 1) Rznomics: The core data is the ORR (efficacy) from the phase Ib/Ila triple combination study of its lead pipeline RZ-001 (hTERT) for primary liver cancer. The existing first-line SoC IMbrave150 showed a final ORR of 27% (benchmark). As this is the first ORR announcement for RZ-001, it will involve interim data (estimated to be primarily low- dose) rather than the full patient group (45 patients), making a direct comparison with IMbrave somewhat ambiguous at this stage. However, confirming an ORR in the low-to- mid 30% range or higher would be considered positive. While market expectations for ORR have recently risen, the fact that this involves low-dose cohorts must be taken into account.

- 2) HLB Innovation: The first validation of the KIR platform (separating activation/inhibition signals) is expected through the SynKIR-110. Since it is a solid tumor CAR-T, toxicity is the most critical point to watch (high affinity is required for high activity in the tumor microenvironment, which increases the potential for toxicity compared to hematologic CAR-T). Attention should be paid to safety indicators, as this is the first clinical data from low-dose administration groups (Cohorts 1-3, with up to Cohort 6 planned for the future).

## AR170's early data competitiveness against ivonescimab

Meanwhile, among South Korean companies presenting pre-clinical data, we recommend drawing attention to Y-Biologics. While pre-clinical abstracts were pre-released on Mar 18, core data will be disclosed on the day of the poster presentation. Y-Biologics plans to present mouse data posters for: 1) AR170 (PD-1xIL-2xVEGF); and 2) AR166 (PD-1xLAG3xIL-2) on Apr 22 at 1:00 AM (KST).

The most important agenda in global anti-cancer drug development this year is whether the next-generation Keytruda (ivonescimab, PD-1xVEGF) receives FDA approval (HARMONi: PDUFA goal date set for Nov 14, 2026). Additionally, immature PFS data for HARMONi-3 (2Q26), immature OS for HARMONi-6 and final OS for HARMONi-2 (mid-2026), and final PFS and immature OS for HARMONi-3 (2H26) are scheduled for release throughout the year. From a data perspective, HARMONi-3 is significant, as it represents the first global head-to- head comparison data against Keytruda (corresponding to China's HARMONi-6).

Meanwhile, China has already moved beyond next-generation Keytruda (PD-1xVEGF) and is already developing the next generation of ivonescimab (trispecific antibody: PD-1xVEGFx new target). Regarding next-generation ivonescimab, CStone’s CS2009 (PD-1xVEGFxCTLA4) is being developed at the fastest pace, with phase I/Il data disclosed in China last March. Safety was favorable with Grade 3 or higher TRAE of 23%, and efficacy was excellent with an ORR of 90% in 1L PD-L1 TPS >50 NSCLC (vs competitors' PD-1xVEGF in the 70% range).

From the perspective of next-generation ivonescimab, we draw attention to the pre-clinical safety and efficacy data of Y-Biologics’ AR170 (PD-1xIL-2xVEGF). As a trispecific antibody that also targets IL-2 (differing from CStone’s CTLA4 target), competitive early data compared to ivonescimab is expected.

- 2. Compass Therapeutics: To present OS and PFS data for tovecimig as second-line bile duct cancer treament

In April, Compass Therapeutics is scheduled to disclose OS and PFS data from the phase II/III trial of tovecimig (ABLOO1) as a second-line treatment for biliary tract cancer. This is significant, as South Korea's ABL Bio is the original developer of tovecimig, and tovecimig is highly likely to become ABL's first commercialized product (expected in late 2026~2027).

Bile duct cancer is a cancer that occurs in the bile duct (bile duct, gallbladder, ampulla), the passage for bile. While the annual number of new patients in the US is relatively small at approximately 25,000, it is a cancer type with steady unmet needs. The biliary tract cancer market targeted by tovecimig consists of patient groups that are:1) unresectable (90%); 2) ineligible for targeted therapy (80% vs HER2, IDH1, FGFR2, BRAF, etc.); and 3) ECOG 0-1 (good systemic condition, 90%). The plan is to first obtain FDA approval as a second-line treatment, and an expansion to first-line treatment may be attempted later depending on the results of investigator-initiated 1L clinical trials at the MD Anderson Cancer Center.

In Apr 2025, top-line data (n=111) for ABLOO1+Paclitaxel from the US phase II/Ill trial (COMPANION-002) in second-line patients ineligible for targeted therapy was disclosed (crossover for the paclitaxel monotherapy group was permitted after disease progression). The ORR was 17.1% (CR 0.9%, PR 16.2%; vs paclitaxel 5.3%, p=0.031) and the DCR was 61.2%, with safety profile remaining consistent with previous studies. That said, the OS data, which is critical for FDA drug review, is scheduled to be released in April. For reference, as the trial permitted crossover between patient groups, rank preserving structural failure time (RPSFT) adjustments will also be presented in the OS interpretation.

The partner company, Compass Therapeutics, has a market cap of approximately W1.5tn based on its core asset tovecimig. Looking at its contract structure with ABL Bio, the remaining milestones include US$96mn for development/approval and US$300mn for commercialization (option: US$110mn if ophthalmic indications are added), with a single- digit royalty rate. For ABL Bio, the value of tovecimig can be recognized as the relative value of Compass's market capitalization. Considering the milestones and the single-digit royalty rate, reflecting around 40~50% of Compass’s market cap appears to be reasonable (presenting the current value of tovecimig attributable to ABL Bio at approximately W600bn~750bn). Following the data announcement in April, the value attributable to ABL will also change according to the fluctuations in Compass's market capitalization.

## 1.EASL: MASH data releases (Hanmi Pharm, D&D Pharmatech)

The European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL, held in May) represents a top-tier global liver disease conference along with the American Association for the Study of the Liver (AASLD, held in November). As for this year’s event, which is scheduled to be held over May 27~30, we draw attention to global MASH data release. While late-breaking abstract titles have yet to be disclosed, we predict MASH phase II data from Hanmi Pharm (partner MSD-efinopegdutide) and D&D Pharmatech (DDO1) will be released.

- 1) Hanmi Pharm’s efinopegdutide: The efinopegdutide phase IIb study (total N=381) will disclose biopsy data at week 52, revealing MASH resolution and fibrosis improvement data for the first time. The study is designed with three drug-administered cohorts and control groups consisting of a placebo and semaglutide 2.4mg (the only global MASH clinical design to directly compare with semaglutide in phase Il). The primary endpoint is the MASH resolution rate, where confirming statistical superiority in a direct comparison with the accelerated-approved semaglutide is most critical (semaglutide ESSENCE phase Ill: 63% at week 72, placebo-adjusted 29%). If it succeeds in proving superiority over semaglutide even in the secondary endpoint, the fibrosis improvement rate, it will be considered a definitive indicator of phase Ill success (semaglutide: 37% at week 72, placebo-adjusted 15%).

Furthermore, indirect comparisons can be made with the phase Il results of survodutide, a leading GLP/GCG candidate. We estimate a MASH resolution rate at 30~40% and a fibrosis improvement rate at approximately 15% (both placebo-adjusted). The value of efinopegdutide attributable to Hanmi is estimated at W1tn (W1.8tn upon success; excluded upon failure).

- 2) D&D Pharmatech’s DDO1: Out of 67 patients on week 48 biopsy, only 35 qualified as evaluable patients (F2-3) following protocol compliance (comparison with placebo). As the data size is small, securing statistical significance against the placebo group, particularly regarding fibrosis improvement, is challenging; thus, the data should be used only to identify trends. That said, even if it fails to secure statistical significance for fibrosis, if it demonstrates the potential for late-stage fibrosis improvement through additional biomarker analysis— similar to Altimmune, entering phase Ill should proceed without difficulty.

- 2. ASCO: The biggest global conference; abstract titles to be disclosed next week

As the world's largest oncology conference, the ASCO annual meeting warrants significant attention as numerous late-stage clinical data sets that could redefine global standard-of- care treatments are presented. This year, it is scheduled to be held from May 29 to Jun 2. While abstract titles of the clinical trials to be presented at the ASCO meeting have yet to be released, both regular and late-breaking abstract titles are scheduled to be disclosed next Tuesday night. Offering a preview of the list of major clinical trials to be presented at ASCO, this event should deserve attention. We predict ASCO-related expectations will grow immediately after the conclusion of the AACR conference next week.

Among domestic pharmaceuticals and bio firms, we draw special attention to Yuhan (MARIPOSA OS) and LigaChem Bio (ROR1 ADC). In particular, the disclosure of the finalized MARIPOSA OS values is of critical importance, as actual Lazcluze prescriptions and sales should increase in earnest only after the release of final OS data. We believe the MARIPOSA presentation is more likely at the ESMO meeting in October, but an ASCO presentation, if it actually happens, will be a significant surprise. We estimate Lazcluze royalty revenue will reach W45.5bn in 2026, based on assumptions of 10% initial royalty rate and W455bn sales (8% penetration rate in the US and 4% in the EU).

Meanwhile, some domestic pharmaceuticals have already disclosed their ASCO presentation topics through the media. Among these companies, we draw attention to TiumBio’s phase lla data on TU2218 as a first-line (1L) treatment for head and neck cancer. The head and neck cancer market is valued at about US$4bn (Keytruda US$3bn, Cetuximab US$500mn, Opdivo US$500mn). Today's 1L treatments are Keytruda monotherapy or combination with chemotherapy, with mOS at approximately one year and ORR limited to the 19~36% range.

Latecomers to the head and neck cancer treatment market are developing combo therapies using Keytruda as a backbone. Leading players include: 1) Merus (phase Ill, petosemtamab: EGFRxLGR4); and 2) Bicara (phase Ill, Ficera: EGFR + TGF-B), followed by TiumBio (TU2218). Merus disclosed phase II data at last year’s ASCO (PD-L1 CPS 1+, 1L ORR 63%; specifically, CPS 1-19 ORR 47%, CPS>20 73%). Later, the European big pharma Genmab acquired Merus for US$10bn (a 41% premium) to secure head and neck cancer assets. Meanwhile, Bicara’s Ficera phase II ORR was 54% (CPS 1-19, consistent ORR above 20), which is inferior to Merus. Nevertheless, Bicara’s market cap ranges at about W2tn.

TiumBio disclosed initial phase Ila data for TU2218 as a treatment of head and neck cancer at SITC last November (total N=12; 1L=8, 2L=4), reporting a very high ORR of 71% (1L: 73%, 2L: 67%). We believe TiumBio’s TU2218 possesses 1L Best-in-Class potential for head and neck cancer and is significantly undervalued considering the valuation of competitors (Merus acquisition price and Bicara market cap). A phase lla update focused on 1L is scheduled at this ASCO, and if a high ORR is maintained, the results could be reflected in valuation.

3.ADA: Focus on survodutide and RNA-based obesity treatments

The American Diabetes Association (ADA) conference is a key venue where major obesity- related data is frequently presented. This year’s event is scheduled for Jun 5~8. As some ADA abstract titles have been released, we explore investment ideas based on survodutide and RNA-based obesity treatments from a global perspective.

Survodutide (GLP/GCG) is a MASH/obesity pipeline under phase Ill trials by Boehringer Ingelheim (original developer: Zealand Pharma), with multiple phase Ill results expected this year. It is considered a global best-in-class candidate for MASH, while a separate phase III trial is underway to explore expansion as an obesity drug. A search of this year's ADA abstract titles confirms that the outcome of Synchronize-1 phase Ill trial to evaluate survodutide for treatment of obesity will be released. This is highly significant as it marks the first phase Ill results for survodutide and suggests its potential as an obesity drug beyond MASH.

In the previous 46-week phase II obesity trial, survodutide recorded a placebo-adjusted weight loss of 16% at the 4.8mg (highest dose) level, but tolerability issues were raised at high doses (4.8mg group: vomiting 35%, treatment discontinuation 29%, Gl-related discontinuation 26%). Accordingly, the pure obesity phase Ill trial (Synchronize-1, 46-week) aims to improve tolerability by increasing the dose more slowly (titration) (phase II: titration every 2 weeks from 0.3mg, 0.6mg, 0.9mg up to 4.8mg vs phase Ill: dose escalation every 4 weeks up to a maximum of 6mg). We foresee that the phase III data will show a weight loss improvement_of around 15% (placebo-adjusted); however, the reduction in discontinuation rates due to tolerability issues should be the most critical point.

In the US, peak sales for survodutide are estimated at around USS$3bn~5bn (sales assumption: obesity 50%, MASH 50%). If superior data (about 15% placebo-adjusted weight loss + increased tolerability) is disclosed at the upcoming ADA, survodutide's peak sales estimate may be revised up. While it is difficult to expect high efficacy due to the same clinical patient profile (low BMI), weight loss of at least 3% would be considered a data surprise.
