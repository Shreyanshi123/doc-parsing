WHY DO WE PROJECT BELOW AVERAGE GROWTH?

We project Novo Nordisk to deliver sales growth below EU biopharma peers 2026-2031 inclusive (Exhibit 1) for two reasons:

1/Semaglutide loss of exclusivity: In 2025, products containing semaglutide (Ozempic, Rybelsus, and Wegovy) accounted

(cagrilintide + semaglutide) over the medium term. Incorporating loss of exclusivity (LOE) in key international markets in 2026
(Canada, Brazil, India, and China), and compound patent expiries in the EU and US in 2031 and 2032 (respectively), we estimate
that 1/3 of group sales are at risk of generic competition in 2026, rising to 2/3 in 2032 (Exhibit 3).

2/Weakening positions in obesity and diabetes markets: Having entered the GLP-1 market for both diabetes (2017,
through Ozempic) and obesity (2021, through Wegovy), Novo’s semaglutide has lost considerable market share to Lilly in both
diabetes (vs Mounjaro) and obesity (vs Zepbound). We think these dynamics are entrenched and see no prospect of a share
reversal. We forecast - 12% group sales growth in 2026 (driven by US price deflation) and then anemic growth across to 2033
(Exhibit 1).

Novo’s Earnings Story so Far: Meteoric Rise, Unfortunate Fall

Why did Novo deliver a sustained period of earnings upgrades? To recap, Novo was an early player in the GLP-1 market,
with Victoza (active ingredient liraglutide) gaining FDA approval in 2010. This was the first once-daily human GLP-1 approved

for type 2 diabetes.

1. GLP1 penetration of US diabetes market. Novo launched Ozempic in the US in 2017, a once weekly GLP-1 for type 2
diabetes management. Since then, GLP- 1s penetration in the US diabetes market has steadily increased (Exhibit 9). In our
view this is primarily because, unlike insulin, these products lead to weight loss rather than weight gain.

2. Inflection of the US obesity market for GLP-1s. The weight-loss benefits enjoyed by patients on Ozempic increased
investors’ expectations of the potential for GLP- 1s to be used as broader weight management medications - with many
patients using Ozempic “off-label” (i.e. for weight-loss and not for diabetes management). Subsequently, data from the
phase 3 SELECT outcomes trial in 3023 inflected perceptions of the potential of GLP-1s in weight loss. This is because in
SELECT Wegovy lowered cardiovascular risk (a composite of reducing heart attacks, strokes, and other CV related deaths)
by 20%. This proved that GLP- 1s deliver medicinal value beyond weight-loss.

What has caused this downgrade cycle? There have been three major events in our view:

¢ Poor execution on manufacturing. We think Novo’s strategy for semaglutide production is a prime study for “failure to

prepare is preparation to fail”. Here is a recap of key events:

¢ Novo let demand for semaglutide products (namely Ozempic and Wegovy) run ahead of their (known) installed capacity
for quality checking and fill-finishing. In March 2022, the FDA announced a shortage of semaglutide, under section 503b.
This led to compounding pharmacies entering the US market to ease the semaglutide shortage.

¢ Novo has invested $25bn since 2022 in capex to bolster fill-finish

(putting the active pharmaceutical ingredient (API)

into pen injection devices). Then, in early 2025, the FDA announced that the semaglutide shortage was over. However,
compounding pharmacies have proven very difficult to remove from the market. Our channel checks indicate are these
drugs still command 1/3 of the US market on a volume basis.

endpoint of non-inferiority (i.e. it was inferior to Zepbound for weight loss). We believe this chain of events has dented
investors’ confidence in Novo’s R&D execution.

WHAT ARE THE PIPELINE MILESTONES?

We expect benign news flow with very few drug catalysts during the next few years (Exhibit 28).

¢ CagriSema: too little, too late. The REDEFINE-4 (CagriSema vs Zepbound head-to-head) headline read out has already
happened (February 2026; click here to be taken to Novo's press room). This means that CagriSema has now delivered
two sets of disappointing phase 3 results (REDEFINE-1 and REDEFINE-4). Hence, we have not included REDEFINE- 11
(longer duration trial with more aggressive dose escalation, read out expected in 1H27) in our catalyst calendar. Moreover,
we now don’t believe that CagriSema can achieve better (or even equivalent) weight loss to Zepbound - regardless of what is
reported in REDEFINE-11.

¢ Amycretin: too early to provide material upside. Novo’s other obesity pipeline prospect has only just entered phase 3
trials. Amycretin has a dual mechanism of action (GLP-1 + amylin analogue) and we acknowledge the phase 2 data thus far
has been solid (Exhibit 44). But we also recognize that Amycretin will be entering a much more crowded obesity market. We
estimate a 2029 launch, by which time there could be 9 major obesity medications on the market (see our obesity market
summary page here).

¢ Ziltivekimab: not differentiated enough. This drug is a fully human monoclonal antibody which targets interleukin 6 (IL-6)
to manage cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, and systemic inflammation. (IL-6 helps cells to communicate
with each other of which one (of many outputs) is regulation of glucose (simple sugar) and lipid (fat) metabolism). But we
understand that there are five other IL-6 inhibitors in phase 3. In addition, Actemra (Roche; O) has the same mechanism of
action but is now facing biosimilar competition so potentially could get used ‘off label’ to manage cardiometabolic conditions.
(Considering that this drug was launched in the US in 2010 it benefits from many years of safe patient use).

Why else aren’t we excited about the late stage pipeline?

In the short term Novo Nordisk intends to launch Mim8 for use hemophilia A. These patients are at risk of (potentially fatal)
uncontrolled bleeding events because they cannot produce enough Factor VIII (protein which facilitates blood clotting) on their
own. Mim8 has the same mechanism of action as Roche’s Hemlibra (launched in 2017; 2025 sales $5bn).

Once administered to hemophiliacs, Hemlibra behaves like naturally occurring Factor VIII (the drug is a mimetic). Mim8 has the
same bispecific antibody mechanism of action (in effect the drug’s ‘two keys’ fit into two different padlocks inside the body) and
was invented by Genmab (UP). Novo in-licensed this drug in 2015.

We acknowledge that from the ‘get go’ Mim8 will be available in a prefilled syringe (PFS) which can be directly injected under
the skin (subcutaneous formulation). Hemlibra is also administered subcutaneously but via vial and syringe so is arguably less
convenient. However, we believe that Hemlibra’s 9 years of safe use represents a formidable barrier to entry to Mim8. We also

12.5x) in 2045. This means that the exit value accounts for just 23% of the total EV.

Could Novo become a single-digit PE stock? Not Quite in our view

In our EV/EBITA valuation, we assume that the stock should trade at a 36% discount to EU peers to account for the portfolio
concentration risk, meek M&A heritage, sustained below average sales growth and poor operational execution.

Compared to EU peers: Our PT implies an adjusted (‘core’) PE26 of 10x for a -40% discount. Meanwhile, in 2030 the implied
discount is -30% (Exhibit 33). Our PT also implies that Novo trades on a 15-year low PE multiple (Exhibit 34). We believe this
is appropriate considering the significant challenge which the company faces in returning to sustainable above sector growth
following the semaglutide US compound patent expiry in 2032 and the company’s weakened competitive positioning in the
obesity and diabetes markets.

Relative to Lilly: our Novo PT implies a -67% discount to Lilly in 2026 and a -53% discount in 2030 (Exhibit 35). This also
implies a 15-year low PE relative to Lilly (Exhibit 36). We believe this is warranted considering Lilly’s far better operational and
R&D execution in metabolic diseases and its superior therapeutic diversification. (Lilly launched its first GLP 1 in diabetes in the
US four years after Novo did).

At our Price Target, Novo would still trade at a premium to Sanofi (O) on PE26 even though the latter’s business model is more

durable as it includes a large vaccines franchise (which, in our view, enjoys the deepest moat in all of biopharma due to the
highest capex, toughest R&D, and no generic risk; Exhibit 37). We thus do not think that our Novo PT is extreme.

US OBESITY MARKET: SOLID VOLUME GROWTH OFFSETTING STICKY DEFLATION

The obesity market is already worth $25bn in 2025. We forecast a 12% value CAGR over the next decade, where the US
obesity market will be worth $85bn (Exhibit 42). So far, this story has been dominated by the share battle between Novo and
Lilly (O; covered by Courtney Breen). But even with new entrants arriving at the end of the decade, we believe the market will
remain a duopolly, with Lilly the dominant player (Exhibit 43).

Our key takeaway from the past five years is simple: in injectables, weight loss wins. Tolerance profile and whether the
drug comes with restrictions (e.g. fasting before taking it) are of secondary importance - headline weight loss is the primary
needle mover. We think this is largely for two reasons.

1. Obesity drugs are prescribed in primary care settings, where practitioners are relatively time poor. Practitioners will
prescribe their “favored” GLP-1 and only move patients onto a different product should their side effects become
intolerable.

2. The direct-to-consumer channel in the US has increased the importance of headline weight loss. Patients seeking to lose
weight (reasonably) want the product which maximizes the speed and magnitude of their weight loss. The tolerance profile
is a “second order” consideration, whereby a degree of discomfort is tolerated for additional weight loss. (Proven ‘outcomes’
benefits such as stroke reduction are even further down the list).

We think the market dynamics are tough to disrupt. We don’t foresee a share reversal back to 2023 or 2024 levels (where
Novo had 60% of the US obesity market). Nor do we think a major disruptor is waiting in the wings. Instead, we think Lilly will
remain the dominant player, Novo the (distant) number two, and a tail of players with smaller shares. In our view, both Lilly and
Novo should benefit from existing positions on the market, which should be protected by pipeline launches.

PRICING: FY26’S DOMINANT THEME

The US price environment has deteriorated faster than expected. Between compounders undercutting, growth in the
direct-to-consumer cash channel, and agreements with the US government, we believe that prices will fall far quicker than is

usual in pharmaceuticals this year. We expect returns to “normal” levels of deflation (10-20% each year) to return from 2027
onwards.

US DIABETES MARKET:

SIMILAR DYNAMICS, SIMILAR CONCLUSIONS
Ozempic is Novo’s largest product by global revenue (Exhibit 85) but we forecast yoy revenue declines even prior
to the US semaglutide LOE (2032). In our forecasts, the cumulative revenues from (global) sales of Wegovy, Oral Sema,
CagriSema (in obesity and diabetes) and all other portfolio and pipeline assets will only just compensate falling Ozempic
revenues (Exhibit 86).

The US Most Favored Nation (MFN) pricing and TrumpRx deals included Ozempic and Mounjaro (Lilly’s GLP-1 for
diabetes). Thus, we expect contractions in the net price of a similar magnitude to the obesity market (which you re-review

here). Ozempic is now available at around $350 per month on TrumpRx and will be available at $250/month via US Medicare
(everyone with Type 2 Diabetes (TD2) >65 years of age eligible) and US Medicaid (all households with income below the Federal
Poverty Level eligible) from April 2026.

We do not believe that the cash channel will be as important in diabetes as it is in obesity. However, we expect lower cash
channel prices (and the lower medicare and medicaid prices) to have spillover effects into the commercial channel. We expect
this to result in a lowering of net US diabetes drug prices. In our detailed bottom up US diabetes model, we forecast -30% US
price declines in FY26,-15% in 2027, and -10% across 2028-36. Similar to the obesity market, 10-20% yoy price declines are
normal in the diabetes segment in the US.

Unlike in Obesity, we don’t model an inflection of GLP-1 prescriptions in 2027, despite the steep price cuts in 2026.

APPENDIX 1: RARE DISEASE

MASH/EFRUXIFERMIN

In 3025, Novo Nordisk confirmed its acquisition of Akero therapeutics for ~$5.2Bn in cash and contingent value rights to
gain access to its lead asset efruxifermin. The drug is a bivalent fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) and beta-klotho receptor
analogue used in the treatment of Metabolic-dysfunction Associated Steato-Hepatitis (MASH). MASH is a disease of the liver
with a high unmet clinical need, and we believe efruxifermin will be a best-in class drug.

What is MASH?

Metabolic-dysfunction Associated Steato-Hepatitis (MASH), previously known as Non-Alcoholic Steato-Hepatitis (NASH), is a
disease process involving the build up of excess fatty tissues within the liver. The fat is deposited through a variety of biological
processes, and can irritate healthy liver cells and cause inflammation and scarring to build up.

Scarring in the liver is more broadly known as fibrosis, or cirrhosis in severe cases which can significantly increase the risk of
liver cancer. MASH can cause fibrosis, and although the two are strongly linked are strictly speaking separate. Hence, one can
exist without the other.

In the US alone, approximately 25% of the population will develop metabolic-dysfunction associated steatotic liver disease

MASH can often be silent with no symptoms, hence the discrepancy between the prevalence of the disease and the number of
patients actually diagnosed and treated.

Those who do have symptoms suffer with tiredness, general weakness, some discomfort in the tummy and occasionally
some itching and bruising. Although by itself it is rarely a debilitating disease, MASH is a stepping stone to more severe
scarring leading to cancer and death. As such, it is considered a serious health problem, and the second leading driver of liver
transplantation worldwide.

Disease burden and treatment paradigm

Given MASH often forms part of a constellation of health problems, estimating its economic impact can be difficult.
Nevertheless, conservative estimates place the medical costs of MASH at $78.6Bn by 2040 in the US alone, whilst MASH
associated productivity loss will be close to $246Bn during same timescale. Per patient medical costs in the US are expected
to be $3,601 by 2040, whilst individual patient productivity loss annually is expected to top $1 1,300 in the same year.
Epidemiological research by Madrigal Therapeutics (NR; a leader in MASH), demonstrates that in addition to a growing
MASH patient population, the percentage of patients being treated will also grow substantially in the medium to long term as
awareness of the disease increases.

To diagnose MASH, physicians may use bloodwork to examine liver function tests, ultrasound scans, and (rarely) a biopsy
(removal of a sample via surgery) of the liver are conducted to confirm the disease pathology. With progress in ultrasound
technology and protocols, the need for biopsy has reduced significantly and clinicians are growing increasingly confident in
treating the disease in the absence of a confirmed tissue sample.

In terms of treatment, a healthy diet and physical activity is often the first line to minimize circulating fats in the bloodstream, and
mitigate the metabolic risk factors contributing to MASH.

Bariatric surgery (forced stomach shrinkage by decreasing the diameter of the gastric tract) to treat obesity is evidenced to be
up to 90% effective in treating MASH. Meanwhile, liver transplantation can be offered in cases of MASH where liver function is
most severely restricted.

Medical (i.e. drug) treatment usually starts with cholesterol lowering medication and good diabetic control. Although medical
treatment specific to MASH is uncommon with only a few approved agents licensed for the disease.

Current therapeutic landscape

Rezdiffra (Madrigal therapeutics; unrated) is a drug that mimics the thyroid hormone to promote fat breakdown and dissolution.
More specifically, Rezdiffra binds to the thyroid hormone receptor beta (THR-beta) in liver fat to promote its breakdown.

In the phase III MAESTRO-NASH trial program, Rezdiffra was compared to placebo to assess fibrosis and MASH on ultrasound
imaging. 25.9% of patients achieved at least a 1 stage improvement in their F score without worsening of MASH compared to
14.2% in the placebo group (p<0.001). No concerning safety signals were reported and the drug was approved as the first
ever licensed treatment exclusively for MASH in 102024. Following sales of $180m in 2024, consensus (Bloomberg) projects
Rezdiffra peak sales of $5Bn in 2031 for a CAGR (25-31e) of 61%.

The second, and only other medication with an indication in MASH is semaglutide, known as Wegovy (Novo Nordisk; O), an
analogue of the glucagon like peptide 1 (GLP-1) receptor. This drug acts on the hormone pathway that promotes appetite
suppression, lowers blood sugar levels and improves control of body weight. Originally indicated for tackling obesity, the
phase IIl ESSENCE study trialed Wegovy against placebo in MASH patients, evaluating whether there was improvement in liver
stiffness and fibrosis reflected on ultrasound imaging and bloodwork.

Competitive landscape

In this exciting time for entrants into the MASH therapy landscape, several other assets are also in phase III which, in our view,

In September 2025 Roche announced the acquisition of 89bio (unrated) for $3.5Bn including contingent value rights for
89bio’s lead asset pegozafermin, an FGF21 analogue designed to treat fatty diseases of the liver, in particular MASH. This
drug’s phase III trials are set to read out in 1H28, and Roche is guiding to >3Bn CHF in nominal peak year sales, its highest

The Main Mechanisms of Action

Obesity drugs, in general, target our “hunger-related” hormones. As the body’s digestive system (and it’s links with the
brain) are extremely complicated, there are numerous hormones needed for the body to function. A drug (such as semaglutide;
active ingredient in Wegovy) may only target a single hormone (and is termed a “single agonist”) others (such as tirzepatide,
active ingredient in Zepbound) target two hormones (“double agonist”). Below we list the main mechanisms of actions:

¢ GLP-1 receptor agonist. Mimics the Glucagon-Like Peptide 1 (a hormone) by inhibiting the release of glucagon (hormone
raising blood sugar levels) and stimulating the production of insulin (a hormone that lowers blood sugar levels) thus reducing
hyperglycemia which leads to lower blood sugar levels. Furthermore, GLP- 1s reduce hunger and slow the Gl (gastro
intestinal) food flow leading to a decrease in appetite.

¢ Amylin receptor agonist. Mimics amylin (hormone secreted with insulin) which inhibits glucagon secretion and affects
gastric emptying plus food intake.

¢ GIP receptor agonist. Mimics the Glucose-dependant Insulinotropic Peptide (hormone) which stimulates insulin production
and sensitivity leading to a better insulin penetration in the bloodstream, thus lowering blood sugar levels. The hormone also
reduces stomach acid production, leading to a slow Gl food flow resulting in a decrease in appetite.

¢ Glucagon receptor agonist. Mimics glucagon which increases blood glucose levels and breaks down lipids and amino
acids. Exogenous intake of glucagon leads to increased energy expenditure with a lower food intake.

¢ CB1 receptor inverse agonist. The peripheral cannabinoid receptor 1 modulates appetite and energy homeostasis but also
metabolic inflammation induced by insulin resistance.

Side Effects and Tolerance

The most common side effects from GLP-1s are gastro-intestinal (Gl) problems. The primary mechanism of action of
GLP-1s is to alter the way our “hunger hormones” behave and interact with each other. It is, therefore, unsurprising that gut-
related issues are the primary side effect from taking GLP- 1s.

e Nausea, vomiting, constipation, bloating, and digestive reflux are all common Gl adverse events. Especially towards
the start of treatment, where the body’s digestive process is undergoing a “re-wiring’, side effects may manifest themselves.
For most people, the side effects are manageable (or tolerated for the benefit of the weight loss).

« Fatigue, headaches, dizziness, and taste changes can also be caused by GLP-1 usage. As the body readjusts to anew
level of caloric intake, the body undergoes a “metabolic stress test”. These side effects can be prominent when people are
adjusting to new dosing levels, and can exacerbated by dehydration (GLP- 1s also suppress the “thirst hormones’).

Whilst we think the tolerance profile of a drug is important, the main driver behind the success of an anti-obesity
product remains headline the weight loss. Patients are willing to accept a worse tolerance profile in order to achieve larger
amounts of weight loss. Most drugs (including those in the pipeline) have a similar discontinuation due to adverse events rates
- certainly not different enough to shape patient behavior. We think the weight loss-tolerance trade off will become more
important as more drugs enter the market, but weight-loss will remain the driving force.

Semaglutide: A Deep(er) Dive

Semaglutide is a long-acting glucagon-like peptide 1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA). Glucagon-like peptide-1 is a naturally
occurring hormone that helps the body regulate blood sugar (glucose) levels, appetite, and the speed at which nutrients are
moved from the stomach into the bloodstream.

Semaglutide is a modified version of the naturally occurring hormone. The GLP- 1 drugs increase the length of time it lasts in the
body and activates the GLP-1 receptors (starting signals) more steadily. Semaglutide has three primary effects on the body:

1. Pancreas: Increases the amount of insulin secreted when glucose is high (normally just after eating) but not when glucose
levels are low (lowering the risk of hypoglycemic - crashes in blood sugar levels - attacks).

2. Stomach: Slows gastric emptying (slows the rate that nutrients enter the bloodstream) which “smooths” out the body’s

glucose response. This lowers strain on the pancreas (reducing the risk of developing diabetes in the first instance, as well
as slowing the pancreatic strain in diabetes patients) and helps patients feel “fuller” for longer.
