Patient numbers continue to grow where they are up 2.5x since 1Q25. Importantly, mgmt emphasized the strength of REZDIFFRA uptake in practice where &gt;42,250 patients are on drug as of March 31st 2026 (vs &gt;36,250 as of YE25). As such, this represents a 2.5x increase since 1Q25 which speaks to the high patient demand for an oral QD liver-targeted therapy such as REZDIFFRA which is both safe/tolerable and effective. With this, mgmt discussed the continued growth of patients on drug demonstrates the utility of "wiring the system" where REZDIFFRA continues to have 1L access establishing it as a foundational MASH therapy. Further, REZDIFFRA offers a differentiated profile supported by strong adherence which enables the continued addition of more patients every quarter. Moreover, mgmt noted this represents a significant achievement by any standard especially in a new market where there is clear unmet need and continued demand from both docs and patients (momentum is strong). Notably, mgmt reminded us the US F2/F3 MASH population is substantially growing where it has increased by ~50% since YE23 and double-digit growth is expected moving forward (Exhibit 1). Accordingly, key drivers of market expansion are overall increased disease awareness, increased diagnosis, and increased patients under specialty care. For reference, mgmt noted NVO (not covered) has helped to grow MASH awareness where MDGL is also seeing more interest from practices in purchasing NITs (to facilitate staging of patients and ability to treat). Given this, mgmt emphasized we are still in the early stages of the MASH market where the diagnosis rate is a little over ~10% and penetration remains under ~10% which sets the stage for rapid expansion and a substantial opportunity to blockbuster peak sales.

Physicians continue to appreciate REZDIFFRA's product profile in MASH where MDGL has made key learnings over the last 2-years. With this, mgmt highlighted physicians (&gt;10K prescribers) strongly appreciate REZDIFFRA's product profile where they have learned what matters most to the MASH market (prescribers, patients, community). Specifically, MDGL walked us through three key aspects of REZDIFFRA's strength (Exhibit 2) which include: 1) best-inclass profile supported by high persistence, prescription depth, and positive market research across prescribers/patients; 2) robust real-world performance bolstered by emerging real-world data and prescribers who detail "my real-world experience has surpassed my expectations";

- and 3) strong leadership where MDGL has the right team, model, and start supported by 93% healthcare provider satisfaction (improved since semaglutide launch), &gt;$1.1B last 12month sales, and &gt;10 pipeline programs. As such, MDGL's strong execution in tandem with REZDIFFRA's leading product profile position it for a continued successful launch with high approval among patients and prescribers. When asked about the physician split, mgmt noted that hepatologists were out of the gate faster/better prepared to use REZDIFFRA in practice, but gastroenterologists outnumber hepatologists (~10:1). Nevertheless, endocrinologists are just starting to come on and are more actively looking for easy to use MASH therapies such as

REZDIFFRA where MDGL considers this a productive specialty with high potential for growth in their future.

Combination of REZDIFFRA and GLP-1 usage continues to be ~25% where MDGL has not observed an impact on REZDIFFRA's launch. When asked about the potential impact of NVO's WEGOVY (semaglutide; MASH approval August 2025) on REZDIFFRA's launch, MDGL indicated that they have observed no real difference or effect on the strength of launch. To support this, MDGL indicated that they are exiting April with their best new-to-brand prescription numbers since beginning REZDIFFRA's launch. Accordingly, mgmt discussed how GLP-1s are becoming a background therapy where many patients are already on a GLP-1 or have had experience with one prior to coming into the clinic for MASH treatment. As such, MDGL noted that ~25% of patients are on a combination of REZDIFFRA and a GLP-1 and ~&gt;50% of patients were previously exposed to a GLP-1. Further, mgmt noted that they expect those numbers will continue to rise given GLP-1's widespread use. Regarding payer dynamics for patients on combination REZDIFFRA and GLP-1, MDGL pointed out that payers will cover a GLP-1 for use in a comorbid indication such as obesity rather than covering both REZDIFFRA and a GLP-1 for the indication of MASH. As a reminder, MDGL's pipeline includes MGL-2086 (oral GLP-1RA small molecule) which has a Ph1 SAD study on track to initiate in 2Q26.

Ph3 MAESTRO-NASH-OUTCOMES data is on-track for 2027. Moreover, MDGL emphasized blinded events are tracking in the range of expectations with topline expected 2027 from Ph3 MAESTRO-NASH-OUTCOMES (NCT05500222; n=845 well-compensated MASH cirrhosis). When asked about fine-tuning guidance, mgmt noted when they have more precision on timing they will provide an update and reiterated events are tracking where they are excited about the results. With this, MDGL has high confidence in the trial considering robust 2-year OLE cirrhosis data which showed REZDIFFRA drives benefit in the cirrhotic (F4c) population. Importantly, these data effectively de-risk Ph3 MAESTRO-NASH-OUTCOMES considering similar patient populations and REZDIFFRA showed benefit across VCTE, CSPH risk (Exhibit 3), Agile-4, spleen volume, MRI-PDFF, CAP, MRE, and lipids with strong safety/tolerability. Specifically, mgmt highlighted 65% of REZDIFFRA patients with CSPH at baseline shifted into a lower-risk category in the 2-year OLE F4c data which effectively supports the potential for REZDIFFRA to treat this high unmet need patient population. In regard to the enrolled patient population, mgmt detailed they have allowed low platelet count patients (≥70K platelet count) in MAESTRO-NASHOUTCOMES with enrichment to make sure they observe outcomes that bring patients back from the cusp of decompensation into a less-severe stage of disease. Moreover, mgmt reminded us there is an addressable population of ~245K US F4cMASH patients who have a greater urgency

to treat given 42x higher risk of liver-related mortality. As such, REZDIFFRA is on-track to be the first-to-market in this indication where there are no treatments currently available.

MDGL further expands pipeline with global licensing agreement with ARWR (covered by Tenthoff) for ARO-PNPLA3, a clinical-stage siRNA. Additionally, MDGL highlighted the recent exclusive global licensing agreement with ARWR for the clinical-stage ARO-PNPLA3. Specifically, ARO-PNPLA3 is a GalNAc-conjugated siRNA designed to reduce expression of the PNPLA3 gene which is highly prevalent among Hispanic MASH patients and represents ~30% of moderate-to-advanced fibrosis patients (consistent with F2/F3). Accordingly, ARWR will receive an upfront $25M payment with additional payments of up-to $975M if certain milestones are achieved and royalties on net sales. In return, MDGL is granted the exclusive global license to develop, manufacture, and commercialize ARO-PNPLA3. With this, it is crucial to appreciate ARO-PNPLA3 already showed Ph1 (NCT04844450) PoC data in n=55 MAFLD patients who were either homozygous or heterozygous carriers of the PNPLA3 I148M variant (~93% Hispanic/ Latino; Fabbrini E, et al. N Engl J Med. 2024 Aug 1;391(5):475-476). For reference, AROPNPLA3 showed (Exhibit 4): 1) up-to -46% reductions in liver fat (MRI-PDFF) at Week 12 following a single-dose at the highest dose tested in PNPLA3 I148M homozygous patients; 2) rapid onset of action with reductions observed as early as Week 6 and sustained response through at-least Week 24; 3) strong safety/tolerability with no clinically meaningful AEs observed;

- and 4) no liver fat content effect observed in heterozygous patients at any of the doses. Of note, a second Ph1 (NCT05039710) in n=9 Japanese patients further support these data. Hence, AROPNPLA3 represents a de-risked clinical-stage asset with clear potential as a precision medicine approach for patients at high-risk of MASH. When asked about what gave them confidence in ARO-PNPLA3 to move forward on this deal, mgmt discussed its high potential to provide complementary efficacy with REZDIFFRA. Specifically, MDGL highlighted their previous data which supported that if there is a greater reduction in liver fat then there is greater efficacy associated with REZDIFFRA. Given that ARO-PNPLA3 was able to drive up-to -46% liver fat reduction with a clean safety/tolerability profile, MDGL believes there is strong potential for additive efficacy with REZDIFFRA in the context of this subpopulation. Additionally, mgmt reiterated this licensing agreement continues to support their growing R&amp;D strategy where they also gained the global rights to six pre-clinical siRNA programs in February 2026 (preclinical development underway).
