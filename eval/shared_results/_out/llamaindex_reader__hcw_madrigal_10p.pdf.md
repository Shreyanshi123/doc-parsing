What's new? Given yesterday's data, we believe the resilience in
patient additions despite seasonality was driven by several key factors.
AI-driven targeting to identify high-value prescribers and predict patient
persistence (as noted by the management during our discussion
post call), and the Patient Services Group actively managing prior
authorization and refill workflows that ultimately determine conversion
rates are potentially some of the key reasons behind the resilience,
in our opinion. This was also the first quarter where Madrigal felt
the full force of seasonal reverifications across the entire patient
base, and we believe those learnings might help optimize future 1Q
performance. Additionally, we think that the endocrinology field force,
just six months in, represents a largely untapped prescriber channel.
The fact that NBRx in April reached their highest level since launch,
even with Wegovy on the market for three quarters, tells us that GLP-1
competition is not taking share from Rezdiffra. On the pipeline, Madrigal
added ARO-PNPLA3, a clinical-stage GalNAc-siRNA with published
Phase 1 data showing 46% liver fat reduction from a single dose in
PNPLA3 I148M homozygous patients, with clean genotype specificity
and durability, which we think significantly de-risks the program. This
targets roughly 30% of F2/F3 patients who face 2x higher event risk,
disproportionately Hispanic, and in our opinion, represents a precision
medicine angle quite differentiated from GLP-1s. Combined with the six
preclinical siRNA targets, Madrigal now has seven genetically targeted
programs addressing non-responders and high-risk subpopulations.
Interestingly, we note that the combination strategy is taking shape
across three distinct forms. Ervogastat and MGL-2086 target broad-
population enhanced efficacy as potential fixed-dose oral combinations,
while the siRNA programs target genotype-defined subgroups as a daily
pill paired with a quarterly-to-semi-annual injectable regimen. To support
the speed of development, we expect Phase 2 studies to be NIT-based
with MRI-PDFF as the primary endpoint, with a clear go/no-go bar, the
combo data must be meaningfully different from Rezdiffra monotherapy
to advance.

How does it alter our thesis and latest thinking? The revenue beat, the best NBRx month since the launch, and the
continued steady patient additions directly validate our view that near-term headwinds are manageable as long-term factors
such as expanding TAM, low penetration, and competitive coexistence remain important factors to be considered as one
continues to think about the commercial program. Most importantly, we like Madrigal’s bullishness in developing combination
therapies which was boosted by the clinical stage PNPLA3 asset acquisition. Management now has three distinct combination
strategies, Ervogastat for broad-population enhanced steatosis reduction, MGL-2086 for pharmacologically inducing the weight
loss that potentiates Rezdiffra's anti-fibrotic effect, and ARO-PNPLA3 for genotype-targeted precision therapy in a high-risk
subpopulation. With respect to the development speed, we think Phase 2 studies might be NIT-based (primarily MRI-PDFF
with fibrosis biomarker panels), and the bar for Phase 3 advancement is that combo data must be "meaningfully different"
from Rezdiffra alone. We also note that the endocrinology expansion is an underappreciated growth driver. With over 10,000
prescribers now and GI outnumbering hepatology 10:1, the endo channel represents a largely untapped patient population,
patients already on background GLP-1 therapy who still present with F2/F3 MASH.
What new questions have emerged? The DTC campaign is a new spending category with no established ROI in MASH,
we need to see whether it's driving patient self-referrals or reinforcing awareness among already-diagnosed patients. Do
endocrinology practices have diagnostic workflows to stage patients and initiate Rezdiffra. On the pipeline side, the key open
question is what endpoints FDA will require for combination approvals?
What work do we anticipate doing? We plan to conduct KOL interviews focused on DTC effectiveness, endocrinologist
receptivity to Rezdiffra, and clinical views on combination trial design, specifically what level of incremental efficacy over
monotherapy would change prescribing practice.
What are we looking for going forward? EASL Congress (May 27-30), a MAESTRO-NASH/NAFLD1 secondary analysis on
Lp(a)/LDL-C reduction (CV-risk read-through, statin-independent) and two real-world efficacy posters with up to 12 months of
follow-up. MGL-2086 Phase 1 SAD initiation in 2Q, first clinical read on the oral GLP-1 strategy. Ervogastat DDI study initiation
in 4Q 2026, confirms whether the fixed-dose oral combination pathway is viable. ARO-PNPLA3 regulatory engagement on
Phase 2 design will signal whether FDA accepts a genotype-restricted approach. MAESTRO Outcomes remains on track for
2027, with positive data opens the nearly 245,000-patient F4c indication and makes Rezdiffra the first fully approved MASH
therapy with outcomes data, well ahead of any competitor.
1Q26 in numbers. The company reported total revenues of $311.3M for 1Q26. Reported R&D expenses were $108.7M
(inclusive of $54.3M in one-time upfront business development expenses), and SG&A expenses were $268.5M, driven by the
endocrinology field force expansion and a new DTC campaign. Net loss was $94.4M, or ($3.25) per share. As of March 31,
2026, the company reported $817.9M in cash, cash equivalents, restricted cash, and marketable securities.
Valuation and Risks. We reiterate our $620 PT and our Buy rating. We estimate the intrinsic value of MDGL in our rNPV model
as the sum of Rezdiffra in MASH with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (F2-F3) in both the U.S. and EU at $596 per share; and
in MASH with compensated cirrhosis in both the U.S. and EU at $34.4 per share (75% PoS). Conservatively, we accrue zero
terminal value beyond the 2033 projection period and assign no value to the combination pipeline (Ervogastat, MGL-2086, ARO-
PNPLA3, or the six preclinical siRNA programs), which represents potential upside to our valuation as clinical data emerge.
Key risks to our rating and PT include: (1) failure to obtain full FDA and EMA approvals across F2-F4c; (2) clinical trial failure or
equivocal data in the MAESTRO-NASH Outcomes study; (3) meaningful delays to our expected timelines, including combination
Phase 2 programs; (4) unforeseen competitive threats from GLP-1-based MASH entrants or other development compounds;
