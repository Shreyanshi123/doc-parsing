4-3 MASH Treatment Market Definition And Segmentations

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), previously known as non-alcoholic steatohepatitis
(NASH), is a chronic and progressive liver disease characterized by fat accumulation, inflammation and fibrosis in the
liver, primarily linked to obesity, insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes and other metabolic disorders. The primary purpose
of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) treatment is to halt or reverse liver damage, prevent
disease progression to cirrhosis or liver failure, and manage associated metabolic conditions. The treatment typically
includes lifestyle interventions, pharmacotherapy, and in some cases, surgical options to reduce liver inflammation,
fibrosis, and metabolic risk factors.

The MASH treatment market consists of sales by entities (organizations, sole traders and partnerships) of MASH
treatment drugs and therapy that is used when lifestyle modifications alone are insufficient to halt or reverse liver
damage. It is utilized across a continuum of care, beginning with non-invasive interventions and progressing to
pharmacological treatment or bariatric surgery in advanced cases."

Complementary interventions include nutritional counseling and physical activity programs, while substitutes may
include broader metabolic treatments that indirectly address the condition. Treatment is administered in clinical
settings such as hospitals and specialty clinics and increasingly through home-based care, especially for oral
medications and telehealth-supported interventions."

Drugs are produced through biopharmaceutical and chemical synthesis processes involving small molecules and
biologics, while therapies may encompass structured weight-loss programs or surgical interventions. These
treatments are distributed through hospital pharmacies for complex or inpatient care, retail pharmacies for
maintenance drugs and online pharmacies for chronic care management.”

Sales occur through both institutional procurement and direct-to-consumer channels, depending on the healthcare
infrastructure and insurance frameworks. Ongoing patient support involves regular liver function testing, imaging
studies, biomarker monitoring and long-term care coordination by hepatologists, endocrinologists and primary care
providers.”

The market covered in this report includes sales of MASH treatment by treatment such as drug and therapy. The
market covered in this report includes sales of MASH treatment for various disease stages such as simple fatty liver /
MASLD (no or mild fibrosis), early MASH (significant/advanced fibrosis), fibrotic MASH, MASH cirrhosis and liver
failure / liver cancer mortality. The main end users of the market include hospitals, specialty clinics, homecare and
other end-users.

This report excludes treatments specifically for liver conditions unrelated to metabolic dysfunction, such as alcoholic
liver disease, viral hepatitis, autoimmune hepatitis and genetic liver disorders. It also excludes diagnostics not directly
associated with treatment, experimental or unapproved therapies. Additionally, it does not include wellness or dietary
supplements marketed without clinical evidence for MASH treatment.

4-4.1 Drug

The drug market consists of sales of pharmaceutical products used to treat MASH by entities that develop and
commercialize medications targeting liver fat reduction, inflammation and fibrosis. These include antifibrotic agents,
GLP-1 receptor agonists, farnesoid X receptor (FXR) agonists, peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR)
agonists, insulin sensitizers, antioxidants and other investigational or approved drugs.”'

The drugs market is sub-segmented by type into antifibrotic agents, antidiabetic drugs, lipid-lowering agents, vitamin
E, obeticholic acid, GLP-1 agonists and other drugs.

4.4.1.2 Antifibrotic Agents

The antifibrotic agents market consists of sales of medications by entities that develop and commercialize drugs
targeting liver fibrosis in MASH. These drugs are designed to reduce or reverse fibrotic tissue buildup, slowing disease
progression and potentially improving liver function in advanced stages of MASH.

4.4.1.2 Antidiabetic Drugs

The antidiabetic drugs market consists of sales of medications by entities that provide glucose-lowering therapies
used to manage MASH. These include insulin sensitizers such as pioglitazone and metformin, which indirectly improve
hepatic steatosis and insulin resistance associated with MASH.“

4.4.1.3 Lipid-Lowering Agents

The lipid-lowering agents market consists of sales of drugs by entities that produce statins, fibrates and other agents
used to manage dyslipidemia in MASH patients. These medications help reduce liver fat accumulation and
cardiovascular risks often associated with MASH.”

4-4.1.4 Vitamin E

The vitamin E market consists of sales of antioxidant therapy by entities providing high-dose tocopherol supplements
used in non-diabetic MASH patients. Vitamin E reduces oxidative stress and hepatic inflammation and is one of the
few non-prescription options recommended for select MASH populations.*

4-4-1.5 Obeticholic Acid (OCA)

The obeticholic acid market consists of sales of FXR agonist drugs by entities that produce and market OCA, a bile acid
analogue developed for reducing liver fibrosis in MASH. OCA is under regulatory consideration or approved in some
