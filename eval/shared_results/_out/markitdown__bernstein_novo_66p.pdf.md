investors’ confidence in Novo’s R&D execution.

WHAT ARE THE PIPELINE MILESTONES?
We expect benign news flow with very few drug catalysts during the next few years (Exhibit 28).
• CagriSema: too little, too late. The REDEFINE-4 (CagriSema vs Zepbound head-to-head) headline read out has already
happened (February 2026; click here to be taken to Novo's press room). This means that CagriSema has now delivered
two sets of disappointing phase 3 results (REDEFINE-1 and REDEFINE-4). Hence, we have not included REDEFINE-11
(longer duration trial with more aggressive dose escalation, read out expected in 1H27) in our catalyst calendar. Moreover,
we now don’t believe that CagriSema can achieve better (or even equivalent) weight loss to Zepbound - regardless of what is
reported in REDEFINE-11.
• Amycretin: too early to provide material upside. Novo’s other obesity pipeline prospect has only just entered phase 3
trials. Amycretin has a dual mechanism of action (GLP-1 + amylin analogue) and we acknowledge the phase 2 data thus far
has been solid (Exhibit 44). But we also recognize that Amycretin will be entering a much more crowded obesity market. We
estimate a 2029 launch, by which time there could be 9 major obesity medications on the market (see our obesity market
summary page here).
• Ziltivekimab: not differentiated enough. This drug is a fully human monoclonal antibody which targets interleukin 6 (IL-6)
to manage cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, and systemic inflammation. (IL-6 helps cells to communicate
with each other of which one (of many outputs) is regulation of glucose (simple sugar) and lipid (fat) metabolism). But we
understand that there are five other IL-6 inhibitors in phase 3. In addition, Actemra (Roche; O) has the same mechanism of
action but is now facing biosimilar competition so potentially could get used ‘off label’ to manage cardiometabolic conditions.
(Considering that this drug was launched in the US in 2010 it benefits from many years of safe patient use).

US OBESITY MARKET: SOLID VOLUME GROWTH OFFSETTING STICKY DEFLATION
The obesity market is already worth $25bn in 2025. We forecast a 12% value CAGR over the next decade, where the US
obesity market will be worth $85bn (Exhibit 42). So far, this story has been dominated by the share battle between Novo and
Lilly (O; covered by Courtney Breen). But even with new entrants arriving at the end of the decade, we believe the market will
remain a duopoly, with Lilly the dominant player (Exhibit 43).
Our key takeaway from the past five years is simple: in injectables, weight loss wins. Tolerance profile and whether the
drug comes with restrictions (e.g. fasting before taking it) are of secondary importance - headline weight loss is the primary
needle mover. We think this is largely for two reasons.
1. Obesity drugs are prescribed in primary care settings, where practitioners are relatively time poor. Practitioners will
prescribe their “favored” GLP-1 and only move patients onto a different product should their side effects become
intolerable.
2. The direct-to-consumer channel in the US has increased the importance of headline weight loss. Patients seeking to lose
weight (reasonably) want the product which maximizes the speed and magnitude of their weight loss. The tolerance profile
is a “second order” consideration, whereby a degree of discomfort is tolerated for additional weight loss. (Proven ‘outcomes’
benefits such as stroke reduction are even further down the list).
We think the market dynamics are tough to disrupt. We don’t foresee a share reversal back to 2023 or 2024 levels (where
Novo had 60% of the US obesity market). Nor do we think a major disruptor is waiting in the wings. Instead, we think Lilly will
remain the dominant player, Novo the (distant) number two, and a tail of players with smaller shares. In our view, both Lilly and
Novo should benefit from existing positions on the market, which should be protected by pipeline launches.

US DIABETES MARKET:
SIMILAR DYNAMICS, SIMILAR CONCLUSIONS
Ozempic is Novo’s largest product by global revenue (Exhibit 85) but we forecast yoy revenue declines even prior
to the US semaglutide LOE (2032). In our forecasts, the cumulative revenues from (global) sales of Wegovy, Oral Sema,
CagriSema (in obesity and diabetes) and all other portfolio and pipeline assets will only just compensate falling Ozempic
revenues (Exhibit 86).
The US Most Favored Nation (MFN) pricing and TrumpRx deals included Ozempic and Mounjaro (Lilly’s GLP-1 for
diabetes). Thus, we expect contractions in the net price of a similar magnitude to the obesity market (which you re-review
here). Ozempic is now available at around $350 per month on TrumpRx and will be available at $250/month via US Medicare
(everyone with Type 2 Diabetes (TD2) >65 years of age eligible) and US Medicaid (all households with income below the Federal
Poverty Level eligible) from April 2026.
We do not believe that the cash channel will be as important in diabetes as it is in obesity. However, we expect lower cash
channel prices (and the lower medicare and medicaid prices) to have spillover effects into the commercial channel. We expect
this to result in a lowering of net US diabetes drug prices. In our detailed bottom up US diabetes model, we forecast -30% US
price declines in FY26,-15% in 2027, and -10% across 2028-36. Similar to the obesity market, 10-20% yoy price declines are
normal in the diabetes segment in the US.
Unlike in Obesity, we don’t model an inflection of GLP-1 prescriptions in 2027, despite the steep price cuts in 2026.
In our view, the GLP-1 segment of the US diabetes market is much more mature than obesity (we are approaching a decade
of Ozempic use in the US; launched in 2017) and is far less geared towards to the “consumer”. Put simply, the pool of patients
actively seeking weight loss is substantially larger than the pool focused solely on tighter glycemic control. Further, we note that
many diabetes patients may wish to use Wegovy (or Zepbound) instead of Ozempic (or Mounjaro) in order to benefit from the
weight loss as well as reductions in HbA1c levels (the active molecule in Zepbound and Mounjaro is the same; tirzepatide).

CAGRISEMA: IT WON’T BE ENOUGH
In the wider GLP-1 market, we think CagriSema can help stabilize Novo’s share loss in the diabetes market. We
understand that Novo will launch CagriSema in diabetes post the launch in obesity (we expect the diabetes launch to be in
2028). In our model, using the same price assumed in our obesity model ($730 - a +100% premium to our assumed blended
net price of Ozempic and Mounjaro) and a similar TRx curve to CagriSema in obesity, we forecast CagriSema to have a peak
share of 3% of the total US diabetes TRx and 7% of GLP-1 TRx (Exhibit 89).
REDEFINE-3 (read out in February 2026) demonstrated that patients taking CagriSema achieved greater reductions
in Hb1Ac than patients using semaglutide. We’re not aware of any head-to-head data published vs tirzepatide yet (and our
understanding is that REDFINE-4 did not have a comparative Hb1Ac reduction as a secondary endpoint), but an indirect (‘meta’)
analysis between REDEFINE-3 and SURPASS-1 suggests to us that Mounjaro (tirzepatide) has more effective Hb1Ac reduction
(Exhibit 90).
All things considered, we think that CagriSema is better diabetes drug than Ozempic, but Mounjaro will remain
the leader. We believe that this is reflected fairly in our 2036 market share forecasts (Exhibit 89). On our estimates, diabetes
revenues will comprise 60-70% of “total” CagriSema revenues (taking the sum of both obesity and diabetes) - Exhibit 91.

APPENDIX 1: RARE DISEASE
MASH/EFRUXIFERMIN
In 3Q25, Novo Nordisk confirmed its acquisition of Akero therapeutics for ~$5.2Bn in cash and contingent value rights to
gain access to its lead asset efruxifermin. The drug is a bivalent fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) and beta-klotho receptor
analogue used in the treatment of Metabolic-dysfunction Associated Steato-Hepatitis (MASH). MASH is a disease of the liver
with a high unmet clinical need, and we believe efruxifermin will be a best-in class drug.
What is MASH?
Metabolic-dysfunction Associated Steato-Hepatitis (MASH), previously known as Non-Alcoholic Steato-Hepatitis (NASH), is a
disease process involving the build up of excess fatty tissues within the liver. The fat is deposited through a variety of biological
processes, and can irritate healthy liver cells and cause inflammation and scarring to build up.
Scarring in the liver is more broadly known as fibrosis, or cirrhosis in severe cases which can significantly increase the risk of
liver cancer. MASH can cause fibrosis, and although the two are strongly linked are strictly speaking separate. Hence, one can
exist without the other.

APPENDIX 2: A GENERALIST’S GUIDE TO GLP-1S: WITH A ZOOM IN ON SEMAGLUTIDE
The Main Mechanisms of Action
Obesity drugs, in general, target our “hunger-related” hormones. As the body’s digestive system (and it’s links with the
brain) are extremely complicated, there are numerous hormones needed for the body to function. A drug (such as semaglutide;
active ingredient in Wegovy) may only target a single hormone (and is termed a “single agonist”) others (such as tirzepatide,
active ingredient in Zepbound) target two hormones (“double agonist”). Below we list the main mechanisms of actions:
• GLP-1 receptor agonist. Mimics the Glucagon-Like Peptide 1 (a hormone) by inhibiting the release of glucagon (hormone
raising blood sugar levels) and stimulating the production of insulin (a hormone that lowers blood sugar levels) thus reducing
hyperglycemia which leads to lower blood sugar levels. Furthermore, GLP-1s reduce hunger and slow the GI (gastro
intestinal) food flow leading to a decrease in appetite.
• Amylin receptor agonist. Mimics amylin (hormone secreted with insulin) which inhibits glucagon secretion and affects
gastric emptying plus food intake.
• GIP receptor agonist. Mimics the Glucose-dependant Insulinotropic Peptide (hormone) which stimulates insulin production
and sensitivity leading to a better insulin penetration in the bloodstream, thus lowering blood sugar levels. The hormone also
reduces stomach acid production, leading to a slow GI food flow resulting in a decrease in appetite.
• Glucagon receptor agonist. Mimics glucagon which increases blood glucose levels and breaks down lipids and amino
acids. Exogenous intake of glucagon leads to increased energy expenditure with a lower food intake.
C
T
U • CB1 receptor inverse agonist. The peripheral cannabinoid receptor 1 modulates appetite and energy homeostasis but also

Side Effects and Tolerance
The most common side effects from GLP-1s are gastro-intestinal (GI) problems. The primary mechanism of action of
GLP-1s is to alter the way our “hunger hormones” behave and interact with each other. It is, therefore, unsurprising that gut-
related issues are the primary side effect from taking GLP-1s.
• Nausea, vomiting, constipation, bloating, and digestive reflux are all common GI adverse events. Especially towards
the start of treatment, where the body’s digestive process is undergoing a “re-wiring”, side effects may manifest themselves.
For most people, the side effects are manageable (or tolerated for the benefit of the weight loss).
• Fatigue, headaches, dizziness, and taste changes can also be caused by GLP-1 usage. As the body readjusts to a new
level of caloric intake, the body undergoes a “metabolic stress test”. These side effects can be prominent when people are
adjusting to new dosing levels, and can exacerbated by dehydration (GLP-1s also suppress the “thirst hormones”).
